    *   **Fever:** Acetaminophen (Tylenol) or Ibuprofen (Advil/Motrin) for fever reduction. Follow dosage instructions carefully.
    *   **Cough:**
        *   **Hydration:** Drink plenty of fluids (water, tea, broth) to thin mucus.

*   **Monitoring:** Monitor symptoms for worsening (e.g., shortness of breath, chest pain, high fever not responding to medication). Seek medical attention if symptoms worsen or persist beyond 7 days.

Test Case 3
Symptoms: ['Joint pain', 'Swelling']
Location: Columbus, Ohio

## Treatment Plan

**1. Medical Actions:**

*   **Diagnosis:** Evaluate for causes of joint pain and swelling, considering osteoarthritis (exacerbated by recent knee replacement and existing arthritis), infection, or other inflammatory conditions. Rule out gout and pseudogout.

*   **Medication:**
    *   **Pain Management:** Acetaminophen (avoiding NSAIDs due to potential interactions and diabetic kidney concerns). Topical analgesics (e.g., capsaicin cream) may be helpful.
    *   **Inflammation Management:** Consider intra-articular corticosteroid injection if pain is severe and localized (after ruling out infection). Discuss risks and benefits.
    *   **Diabetes Management Review:** Ensure optimal blood sugar control, as this can impact inflammation and healing. Review current insulin regimen.

*   **Physical Therapy:** Initiate a physical therapy program to improve range of motion, strength, and joint stability. Focus on exercises that are appropriate post-knee replacement and consider mobility issues.

*   **Lifestyle Modifications:**
    *   Weight management (if applicable).
    *   Low-impact exercise (e.g., walking, swimming).
    *   Dietary modifications to reduce inflammation (e.g., Mediterranean diet).

*   **Monitoring:**
    *   Monitor blood glucose levels regularly.
    *   Follow up with physician in 1-2 weeks to assess pain and swelling.
    *   Monitor for any signs of infection (especially around the knee replacement).

**2. Location-Specific Options:**

*   **Telehealth Option:** Christ Hospital (Cincinnati, OH) offers telehealth and is within reasonable proximity if in-person follow-up is needed. Consider this for initial consultation and medication management review.
*   **In-Person Option:** Mount Carmel East (Columbus, OH) is the closest hospital and provides in-person services if a physical examination or intra-articular injection is deemed necessary.

**3. Justifications:**

*   **Acetaminophen:** First-line pain reliever, preferred due to sulfa allergy and potential risks of NSAIDs with diabetes.
*   **Topical Analgesics:** Non-systemic option for localized pain relief.
*   **Intra-articular Corticosteroid Injection:** Can provide rapid pain relief if localized and other measures fail. Requires careful consideration due to potential risks and benefits.
*   **Physical Therapy:** Crucial for restoring function and preventing further joint damage.
*   **Diabetes Management:** Optimal glucose control is essential for healing and reducing inflammation.
*   **Telehealth First:** Convenient for initial assessment and medication review, minimizing travel burden.
*   **Local Hospital Consideration:** Mount Carmel East is the closest option for in-person care, should it be needed.
